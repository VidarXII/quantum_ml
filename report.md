Mathematical FormulationTo ensure the reconstructed density matrix $\rho$ is Positive Semi-Definite (PSD) and has a unit trace, we constrained the model outputs using the Cholesky decomposition:$$\rho = \frac{L L^\dagger}{\text{Tr}(L L^\dagger)}$$Model performance was evaluated using Quantum Fidelity. For a pure target state $|\psi\rangle$, the fidelity simplifies to:$$F(\rho, \psi) = \langle \psi | \rho | \psi \rangle$$For mixed states, the generalized fidelity formula was used:$$F(\rho, \sigma) = \left( \text{Tr} \sqrt{\sqrt{\rho} \sigma \sqrt{\rho}} \right)^2$$Results SummarySingle Qubit (Transformer): Achieved a mean fidelity of $>0.98$ on the test set, successfully mapping Pauli expectation values to valid density matrices.Scalability Benchmark: Initial untutored fidelity drops exponentially as $1/2^N$, validating that random guessing fails beyond $N=4$.Final Reflection: Scaling Limits & Future WorkOur scalability study revealed a strict $O(2^{3N})$ runtime bottleneck caused by the matrix multiplication of the full-rank Cholesky factors ($L L^\dagger$). While the GPU handles up to 10 qubits easily, the memory and compute limits are hit sharply at 12 qubits.Additionally, ablation studies on initialization variance revealed that the Cholesky ansatz is scale-invariant due to the trace normalization step. Future improvements should focus on Low-Rank Approximations (learning an $N \times r$ matrix where $r \ll 2^N$) or integrating Classical Shadows to estimate observables without reconstructing the full exponentially large density matrix.
